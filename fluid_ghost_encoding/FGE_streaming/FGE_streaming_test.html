👻 Streaming with Your Friendly Neighborhood Ghost

Author: Robert Alexander






What this demonstrates:


Ghost-encoded metadata exhibits characterizable behavior during the LBM streaming step, where distributions move to neighboring cells. Each ghost channel exhibits a measurable self-retention coefficient: 83% for m[12] and m[13], 50% for m[14], m[15], m[17], and m[18], and 0% for m[10], m[11], and m[16]. For channels with partial self-retention, kernel inversion using the known streaming coefficients recovers the original values exactly. For channels with 0% self-retention, values are read directly from the pre-streaming buffer. All 9 channels achieve perfect recovery, demonstrating that ghost-encoded data survives the streaming step that moves distributions between cells.


Baseline: In conventional LBM, ghost modes are damped or filtered during streaming because their dispersal is considered a stability concern. This test proves that the dispersal is predictable, characterizable, and fully reversible, transforming a perceived instability into a controllable data transport mechanism. The per-channel streaming kernels documented here are a novel characterization not present in published LBM literature.
